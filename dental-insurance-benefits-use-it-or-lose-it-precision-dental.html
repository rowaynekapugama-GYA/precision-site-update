How Dental Extras Cover Actually Works

4 November 2021·7 min read

Private health extras cover is one of those things most of us hold without fully understanding. We pay the premium, hand over the card at the front desk, and hope the gap is small. Then somebody mentions that benefits reset at the end of the year, and a mild panic sets in.

Here is a clear explanation of how extras cover works for dental treatment, what the common terms mean, and how to make sensible decisions with it, rather than rushed ones.

Annual Limits and Why They Reset

Most extras policies include an annual limit for general dental, and often separate limits for major dental and orthodontics. The limit is the maximum the fund will pay towards those services in a benefit year.

For most funds the benefit year runs to 31 December, although some align with the financial year. Unused benefits generally do not carry over into the next year. If you have not used your general dental limit and you are due for a check-up and clean, there is no reason to leave it until January.

A routine visit is also how early problems, such as decay, gum disease, gum recession and changes that need checking for oral cancer, are picked up while they are simpler to manage. That is the sensible version of "use it or lose it". The unhelpful version is treating your limit as a budget that must be spent. Dental treatment should be based on clinical need, not on the calendar, and no reputable practice will recommend work simply because you have benefits remaining. If nothing needs doing, nothing needs doing.

The Terms You Will Encounter

General and Major Dental

General, sometimes called preventive, usually covers examinations, cleans, X-rays, fluoride, simple fillings and simple extractions. Major dental usually covers crowns, bridges, dentures, root canal therapy and some surgical work. The two often have separate limits and different rebate percentages.

Waiting Periods

New policies and upgrades usually carry waiting periods. General dental commonly has a short wait, while major dental often has twelve months. If you are considering significant treatment, it is worth checking your waiting periods early rather than at the point of booking.

Percentage Rebates and Sub-Limits

Many policies pay a percentage of the fee rather than a fixed amount, and some apply sub-limits to particular item numbers, such as a separate cap for crowns. Two people with the same limit can therefore receive quite different rebates. On family policies, limits often apply per person, so each family member may have their own general dental allowance.

Excesses and Co-Payments

Some extras policies apply a co-payment or annual excess, which resets with the benefit year. If you have already met it this year, completing planned treatment before the reset can reduce your out-of-pocket cost. Your policy documents or your fund can confirm whether this applies.

Preferred Providers

Funds negotiate arrangements with certain practices. Precision Dental is a preferred provider for Bupa, HCF and CBHS, which can mean a higher rebate for members of those funds, depending on their level of cover. We accept most other major funds as well.

HICAPS

HICAPS lets us process your claim at the practice at the time of your appointment. Your fund pays its share directly and you pay only the gap, so there is no paperwork to lodge afterwards.

How to Find Out What You Are Covered For

The most reliable approach takes about ten minutes:

  • Log in to your fund's app or website and look up your remaining annual limits
  • Check your waiting periods, particularly for major dental
  • Ask us for the item numbers for any planned treatment
  • Call your fund with those item numbers and ask what they will pay

Our team is happy to provide item numbers and to talk through what we have seen with other patients on similar policies, but only your fund can confirm your entitlement.

Planning Treatment Across Two Benefit Years

Where a treatment plan is significant, staging it across two benefit years sometimes makes financial sense, provided it is clinically appropriate to do so. A plan might begin with the most urgent work before the end of the year, with the remainder scheduled after limits reset.

What should never happen is delaying urgent treatment purely to suit a benefit year. Infection, active decay and progressing gum disease do not wait, and the cost of delay usually exceeds the rebate gained. A small cavity that could be filled this year can progress to needing root canal therapy if it is left. Where a plan can reasonably be staged, we will say so. Where it cannot, we will say that too.

If You Do Not Have Extras Cover

Plenty of people do not, and there are other ways to manage cost. Payment plans through DentiCare let eligible patients spread treatment into regular payments with no deposit and no interest, subject to eligibility, terms and conditions. Eligible children and teenagers may have access to benefits under the Child Dental Benefits Schedule. For major treatment such as dental implants, some patients explore early release of superannuation, which has its own eligibility rules and is worth discussing with a financial adviser.

Our Prices and Payment Options page sets out the methods we accept and the plans available, and we always provide a written quote before treatment begins.

A Sensible Way to Use Your Cover

  • Book your routine check-up and clean rather than leaving benefits unused
  • Ask for item numbers before major treatment and confirm rebates with your fund
  • Treat urgent problems, such as gum and periodontal treatment, promptly regardless of your limit
  • Stage elective work around benefit years only when it is clinically reasonable
  • Keep children's visits regular, since prevention is usually the best value of all

Fees and the New Year

Many practices, like many businesses, review their fees at the start of a calendar year. If you already have treatment planned, it is reasonable to ask whether the quoted fees will change after January, so you can factor that into your timing.

Understanding Your Quote

A written quote makes health fund conversations much easier, because it lists the item numbers involved. Item numbers are the standard codes funds use, and they are what determines the rebate, not the description of the treatment.

A quote should show each item number, the fee for it, and the total. With that in hand, your fund can tell you exactly what it will pay, and you can calculate your gap before committing to anything.

If a treatment plan is staged, ask for it to be broken down by stage, so you can see what falls in each benefit year. We are happy to prepare quotes this way, and there is never any obligation to proceed after receiving one.
