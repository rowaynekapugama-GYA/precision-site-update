Why a Professional Clean Does Something Brushing Cannot

2 July 2019·6 min read

"Why do I need a clean if I brush twice a day?" is a fair question, and the answer is specific rather than vague. There is a physical substance on your teeth that a toothbrush cannot remove, and its presence drives most gum disease.

How Tartar Forms

Plaque is a soft film of bacteria that begins re-forming on your teeth within hours of brushing. Where it is removed daily, it causes no lasting harm.

Where it is left, minerals from saliva begin to crystallise within it, and in a matter of days it hardens into calculus, commonly called tartar. Tartar is firmly attached to the tooth surface and cannot be brushed off. It also has a rough surface that attracts more plaque, so the problem compounds.

The areas where it forms first are predictable: behind the lower front teeth and around the upper back teeth, near the openings of the salivary glands.

Why It Matters

Tartar holds bacteria against the gum continuously. The gum responds with inflammation, which is gingivitis: redness, puffiness and bleeding when you clean. At this stage the condition is reversible.

If inflammation continues below the gum line, the attachment between gum and tooth breaks down and pockets form, which are impossible to clean at home. Bone supporting the tooth is gradually lost, and that loss is permanent. This is periodontitis, and it is the leading cause of tooth loss in adults.

The progression is usually painless, which is why regular examination matters as much as the cleaning itself.

What Happens During the Appointment

A professional clean is normally combined with an examination as part of a check-up and clean.

  • Assessment. We check your gums, measure pocket depths and record bleeding points, so changes can be compared over time.
  • Scaling. An ultrasonic instrument uses fine vibrations and water to break tartar away from the tooth, with hand instruments used for finer work and areas just below the gum line.
  • Polishing. A soft rubber cup and paste smooth the surfaces and lift surface stain from coffee, tea and other sources.
  • Flossing. The spaces between teeth are cleared of any remaining debris.
  • Fluoride. Often applied afterwards to strengthen enamel or reduce sensitivity.

Most cleans are comfortable. If your gums are inflamed or your teeth are sensitive, parts of it may feel tender, so tell us and we will adjust the approach, work in sections or discuss anaesthetic.

When a Standard Clean Is Not Enough

Where pockets have formed and there is tartar well below the gum line, a routine clean cannot reach it. In that case we recommend gum and periodontal treatment, which is a deeper, more thorough procedure usually carried out with local anaesthetic over one or more appointments, followed by a review to see how the tissues have responded.

This is a different treatment with a different fee, and we will explain why it is recommended rather than simply booking a longer clean.

How Often

Six months suits many people, but the interval should reflect your own risk. More frequent visits, often every three or four months, are commonly recommended for people with a history of periodontitis, heavy tartar build-up, diabetes, dry mouth or who smoke. Others with consistently healthy gums can safely go longer.

Your preventive care for adults plan should be based on what we actually find in your mouth over time, not a fixed rule.

What a Clean Will and Will Not Do

It removes plaque, tartar and surface stain, and it leaves teeth feeling smooth. It often makes teeth look brighter, because stain is lifted.

It does not change the natural internal colour of your teeth, which requires professional teeth whitening. It also does not fix wear from grinding and clenching, sensitivity from recession or decay, all of which need separate attention.

What You Can Do Between Cleans

A professional clean resets things, but what happens in the months between determines how much tartar has re-formed by the next visit.

Two habits matter most: two minutes of brushing twice daily with a soft brush angled at the gum line, and cleaning between the teeth every day. Tartar forms first in the places people brush quickly, so give extra attention to the inner surfaces of the lower front teeth and the outer surfaces of the upper back teeth.

Bleeding when you clean is worth paying attention to rather than avoiding the area. It usually settles within a week or two of consistent cleaning, and if it does not, tell us at your next visit.

Sensitivity After a Clean

Mild sensitivity for a day or two after a clean is common, particularly where tartar has been covering exposed root surfaces or where gums were inflamed. It settles as the tissues recover.

A desensitising toothpaste used twice daily helps, and it works best when it is not rinsed away immediately, so spit rather than rinse. Avoid very hot or cold foods for a day if the teeth feel reactive.

If sensitivity persists beyond a couple of weeks, tell us, as there may be a specific cause such as recession, a worn area at the gum line or a cracked tooth that is worth addressing directly.
