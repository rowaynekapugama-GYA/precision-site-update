Oral Hygiene at Home: What Actually Matters

30 September 2022·6 min read

Most people brush twice a day and assume that covers it. Brushing is essential, but it only reaches around three of the five surfaces of each tooth, which is why the areas between teeth account for so much of the decay and gum disease we see.

Good home care is not complicated, and it does not require a cupboard full of products. It comes down to doing a few things properly and consistently.

Brushing Well

Technique Beats Effort

Scrubbing hard does not clean better. It wears enamel and contributes to gum recession, exposing sensitive root surfaces. Use a soft-bristled brush, angle it at roughly 45 degrees towards the gum line, and use small circular or short back-and-forth movements. Let the bristles do the work.

Two Minutes, Twice a Day

Two minutes is longer than it feels. Divide your mouth into quadrants and give each around 30 seconds, making sure you reach the inner surfaces and the back teeth, which are commonly rushed.

Manual or Electric

Both work well when used correctly. Electric brushes, particularly oscillating-rotating types, can make good technique easier, and built-in timers help. If you use one, guide it slowly along each tooth rather than scrubbing with it.

Spit, Do Not Rinse

After brushing, spit out the excess toothpaste but avoid rinsing with water. Rinsing washes away the concentrated fluoride that would otherwise keep working on your enamel.

Timing Around Acid

If you have had something acidic, such as citrus, soft drink, wine or coffee, rinse with water and wait at least 30 minutes, and up to an hour, before brushing, as enamel is temporarily softened.

Cleaning Between Your Teeth

This is the step most often skipped and the one that changes gum health the fastest.

  • Floss suits tight contacts. Use a length you can control, curve it into a C shape around each tooth and slide it gently below the gum line, rather than snapping it down.
  • Interdental brushes are easier for many people and are often more effective where there is space between teeth. Size matters, and we can help you work out which sizes fit your gaps.
  • Water flossers are a reasonable option for people with bridges, implants or braces, or anyone who finds the alternatives difficult.

Once a day is enough. Bleeding when you start is common if the gums are already inflamed, and it usually settles within a week or two of consistent cleaning. If it does not, have your gums assessed, as it may indicate gum and periodontal treatment is needed.

Your Tongue

The back of the tongue holds bacteria and debris and is a leading contributor to bad breath. Clean it gently once a day with a tongue scraper or the back of your brush, working from back to front.

Toothpaste and Mouthwash

Fluoride is the active ingredient that matters most, so check it is present. Beyond that, choose based on your needs: a desensitising paste for sensitivity, a high-fluoride paste if we have recommended one for high decay risk, or a low-abrasion paste if you have worn enamel. Whitening toothpastes remove surface stain but do not change the natural colour of teeth, which requires professional teeth whitening.

Mouthwash is optional. If you use one, choose alcohol-free, and use it at a different time from brushing so it does not wash away fluoride. Antibacterial rinses are useful for short periods when recommended, but are not intended for indefinite daily use.

Habits That Help as Much as Cleaning

  • Water as the default drink. Frequent sugary or acidic drinks undo good brushing.
  • Fewer snacking occasions. Frequency matters more than quantity, because each exposure restarts the acid cycle.
  • Chew sugar-free gum after meals to stimulate saliva when you cannot brush. Gum sweetened with xylitol is a good choice, as the bacteria that cause decay cannot use it.
  • Do not smoke or vape. Both affect gum health and mask early warning signs such as bleeding.
  • Protect your teeth at night if you grind. A splint for grinding and clenching prevents wear that home care cannot fix.
  • Replace your brush or brush head every three months, or sooner when the bristles splay. Worn bristles clean poorly, and an old brush also harbours bacteria.

Helping Children Build the Habit

Children need help with brushing until they can do it well themselves, usually around age eight. Use a smear of children's fluoride toothpaste for under-threes and a pea-sized amount after that, brush twice daily, and start cleaning between teeth as soon as two teeth touch. Regular children's dentistry visits reinforce the message and pick up problems early.

What Home Care Cannot Do

Once plaque hardens into tartar, brushing will not remove it, and it continues to hold bacteria against the gum. That is the job of a professional check-up and clean, alongside the examination that spots what you cannot see. Home care and professional preventive care for adults work together; neither replaces the other.

Common Mistakes Worth Correcting

A few habits come up repeatedly, and they are easy to fix once you know.

Brushing straight after breakfast, particularly after juice or citrus, works against you. Brush before breakfast, or wait half an hour afterwards.

Using a medium or hard brush in the belief it cleans better. It does not, and it wears enamel and gums over the years.

Keeping a brush head long past its useful life. Splayed bristles clean poorly.

Flossing only when something is stuck. Cleaning between teeth is a daily preventive habit, not an occasional rescue.

Relying on mouthwash instead of mechanical cleaning. Rinsing does not disrupt the plaque biofilm; brushing and interdental cleaning do.
