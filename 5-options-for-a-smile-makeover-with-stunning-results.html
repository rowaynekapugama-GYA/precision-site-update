5 Smile Makeover Options Worth Considering

15 June 2022·6 min read

A smile makeover is not one treatment. It is a plan, built from whichever options suit your teeth, your gums and what you would like to change. For one person that might mean a single appointment with a whitening tray. For another it could mean several visits over a few months.

The starting point is always the same, though. Before anything cosmetic happens, your teeth and gums need to be healthy. Decay, gum inflammation and heavy grinding all affect how well cosmetic work lasts, so we address those first. Once your mouth is in good shape, here are five of the options we most often discuss with patients in Fortitude Valley.

1. Professional Teeth Whitening

Teeth naturally darken with age, and coffee, tea and red wine leave their mark. Professional teeth whitening lightens the natural colour of your teeth using gels applied under dental supervision, either in custom-made take-home trays or in the chair.

It is often the least invasive place to start, because nothing is removed from the tooth. It also helps set your target shade before other work begins, since fillings, crowns and veneers do not change colour with whitening. Whitening is not permanent, and results vary depending on the type of staining and your habits afterwards. Some people notice temporary sensitivity during treatment.

2. Composite Bonding and Composite Veneers

If a front tooth is chipped, slightly short or a little out of shape, tooth-coloured composite resin can be sculpted directly onto it and polished to blend with the surrounding teeth. Composite resin veneers usually need little or no enamel removed, and the work is often completed in one appointment.

Composite is more prone to staining and wear than ceramic, so it typically needs polishing over time and eventual replacement. In return, it is more conservative, easier to repair and usually costs less than porcelain.

3. Porcelain Veneers

Porcelain veneers are thin, custom-made ceramic shells bonded to the front of the teeth. They can change colour, shape, length and proportion together, which makes them a common choice when several front teeth are involved.

There is an important trade-off. A thin layer of enamel is usually removed to make room for the veneer, and that change cannot be undone, so the tooth will always need a veneer or another restoration. Veneers can chip or wear and may need replacing over the years. If you grind your teeth, a protective night splint is usually part of the plan.

4. Clear Aligners

Sometimes the issue is not the colour or shape of individual teeth, but how they sit together. Crowding, gaps and teeth that have drifted since childhood braces can all be addressed with Invisalign clear aligners, a series of removable trays that move teeth gradually.

Straightening teeth first often means less restorative work later, because teeth in better positions may not need reshaping to look even. Aligners need to be worn around 20 to 22 hours a day, so the result depends heavily on how consistently you wear them, and retainers are needed afterwards to hold the new position.

5. Crowns, Bridges and Implants

Where teeth are heavily filled, cracked, worn down or missing, cosmetic improvement and structural repair go hand in hand. A ceramic crown restores the shape and strength of a damaged tooth, and with CEREC many crowns can be designed and fitted in a single visit. A bridge replaces a missing tooth using the neighbouring teeth for support, while dental implants replace the tooth root itself and carry a crown of their own.

These options involve more clinical work than whitening or bonding, and each carries its own risks and maintenance requirements, which we go through in detail before you decide.

How a Plan Comes Together

Most makeovers combine two or three of these options rather than relying on one. A typical planning appointment involves:

  • A full examination, including digital X-rays, and a check of your gum health and bite
  • An assessment of how your teeth sit in relation to your face: the line of your lips when you smile, how much gum shows, tooth proportions and the shade that suits your colouring
  • Photographs, and in some cases digital scans, so we can look at your smile in relation to your face
  • A conversation about what bothers you, what you would like to change and what you would rather leave alone
  • A written treatment plan with staged options and expected costs

Where it helps, we use Digital Smile Design to show you proposed changes on screen before anything begins. It is a visualisation, not a promise, and we are always clear about that distinction.

Questions Worth Asking Before You Start

Take your time with a cosmetic decision, and ask about:

  • The long-term maintenance each option needs
  • What happens if a restoration chips or fails
  • Whether a more conservative option would achieve enough of what you want
  • Whether your grinding habits or gum health might affect the result
  • The full cost, including reviews and any protective appliances

A good plan should still make sense to you a month after the consultation.

Looking After the Result

Whatever combination you choose, the care afterwards is much the same. Brush twice daily with a soft brush, clean between your teeth every day, keep up regular check-ups and cleans, and wear a night splint if grinding is a factor. These habits protect both your natural teeth and the work you have invested in.

We will examine your teeth and gums, explain the realistic options and let you decide in your own time.
