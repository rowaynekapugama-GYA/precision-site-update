Tooth Extraction in Fortitude Valley

Our goal is always to save natural teeth wherever possible. However, there are times when removing a tooth is the most appropriate choice for your oral health. When an extraction is needed, we take a careful, gentle approach and make sure you understand what to expect before, during and after treatment.

What Is a Tooth Extraction?

A tooth extraction is the removal of a tooth from its socket in the jawbone. A simple extraction is performed on a tooth that is visible in the mouth, using instruments to loosen and gently remove it. A surgical extraction may be needed for teeth that are broken at the gum line, have curved roots or have not fully erupted, and it can involve a small incision in the gum.

Is a Tooth Extraction Right for You?

An extraction may be recommended when:

  • A tooth is too badly decayed or broken to be restored
  • Advanced gum disease has loosened a tooth
  • A tooth has a severe infection and root canal therapy is not suitable or preferred
  • Wisdom teeth are impacted or causing problems
  • Teeth are crowded and removal is part of an orthodontic plan
  • A baby tooth is not falling out as expected

Before recommending an extraction, we explore alternatives, such as root canal therapy or a crown, and discuss the benefits, risks and costs of each option. We also talk about replacing the tooth, if needed, with a dental implant, dental bridge or dentures.

What Happens at Your Appointment

Assessment

We examine the tooth, review your medical history and medications, and take digital X-rays to assess the roots and surrounding structures. Please let us know if you take blood-thinning medication or have any medical conditions.

Keeping You Comfortable

Local anaesthetic is used to numb the tooth and surrounding area. If you feel nervous, happy gas can help you relax.

The Extraction

The tooth is gently loosened and removed. You may feel pressure, but you should not feel sharp pain. If a surgical extraction is required, stitches may be placed.

Recovery Instructions

We place gauze over the area and provide clear written aftercare instructions before you leave. In some complex cases, referral to an oral surgeon may be recommended.

Aftercare and Ongoing Care

Bite firmly on the gauze for 30 to 60 minutes to help a blood clot form. For the first 24 hours, avoid rinsing, spitting, drinking through a straw, smoking, alcohol and strenuous exercise, as these can dislodge the clot. Eat soft foods, chew on the other side and use a cold compress to reduce swelling. From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals. Contact us if you experience severe or worsening pain after a few days, persistent bleeding, fever or swelling that increases.

Why Choose Precision Dental for Tooth Extractions

We take time to explain why an extraction is recommended and what alternatives are available. Our team works gently and at your pace, offers happy gas for anxious patients, and discusses tooth replacement options so you can plan ahead.

Fees, Health Funds and Payment Options

The cost of your treatment depends on your individual needs. After your examination, we talk you through your options and the expected fees before any treatment begins. We accept most major health funds, process claims on the spot through HICAPS and are a preferred provider for Bupa, HCF and CBHS. Interest-free payment plans are available through DentiCare for eligible patients, subject to terms and conditions. You can learn more on our Prices and Payment Options page.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Local anaesthetic numbs the area, so you should feel pressure but not sharp pain. Some discomfort after the anaesthetic wears off is common.

Most people feel more comfortable within a few days. The gum usually heals over a couple of weeks, while the bone takes longer.

A dry socket occurs when the blood clot is lost from the extraction site, exposing the bone. It can be painful and should be treated by your dentist.

Once the anaesthetic has worn off, you can eat soft foods. Avoid hot drinks, very hard foods and chewing on the extraction site for the first few days.

Replacing a missing tooth can help prevent neighbouring teeth from shifting. Options include implants, bridges and dentures, which we can discuss.

Many people return to work the next day. For surgical extractions, we may recommend taking a day or two to rest.
